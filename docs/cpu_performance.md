# CPU fitting in Steps 3 and 4

Both tabs expose the same **Blur / gain fitting method** choice:

| Choice | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Blur then gain (fastest)** — default | Find blur with uniform gain, keep that blur fixed, then fit the spatial gain. |
| **Blur then gain + joint refinement** | Start with the fast fit, then refine blur and gain together. Keep the staged result if refinement would increase its reconstructed residual error. |
| **Joint blur and gain** | Search blur and gain together from the outset. |

The choice applies to selected-point fits, ROI fits and combined Steps 2–3/2–4 runs. It is saved in workflows and recorded in Step 4 export settings. Older workflows without a saved choice start with the fastest method. Changing a method affects subsequent fits; existing fitted results retain their parameters until recomputed.

Step 3 eliminates the two gain amplitudes with a small weighted least-squares solve when the gain bounds permit it. Custom gain bounds use a bounded search so the fitted parameters remain within the requested limits. Step 4 preserves one shared gain map and nonnegative mixture amplitudes; it does not independently fit gain maps for the two components. Its original order of blurring, applying gain and normalizing is preserved.

CPU objectives reuse coordinates, weights and exact blur values, score candidates from weighted moments, and construct final images only for the selected fit. Final residuals and mixture fractions use the existing image equations. The nonlinear search uses float64 internally; residual storage and exported reconstruction remain float32. Numerical edge cases and custom bounds are covered by regression tests.

H5OINA selection uses verified direct reads for small batches and a chunked lazy view for larger selections, avoiding reconstruction of a full-map Dask graph for each batch. Ordering, duplicate indices, conditioning, cropping and software binning are preserved. Worker projections batch orientations while retaining individual PCs; each worker materializes its selected master energy once.

Routine ROI results retain fit metadata in memory. Step 3 residual images use a session-owned, lossless float32 HDF5 cache in the system temporary directory. Inspection retains at most four full results per step, plus references held by the current view. The cache allocates only written patterns and is cleared on invalidation, session replacement and normal shutdown. A complete 95,340-point scan at 128 × 156 pixels needs approximately 7.6 GB of temporary residual image storage instead of keeping approximately 45.7 GB of six-plane diagnostic images in RAM. Saved workflows retain the fit metadata and reconstruct images after reopening; the temporary cache is not a permanent export.

## Measured validation

Measurements used the supplied Cu reference workflow on an M4 Pro, 128 × 156 patterns, no mask/background subtraction, and the saved limits of 80 generations and population multiplier 15. Reference code is commit `301ba4d`. Single-run samples are useful comparisons, not full-map throughput guarantees.

| Measurement | Previous implementation | New default |
|---|---:|---:|
| Step 3 residual construction, same 80 points and 10 cores | 12.99 s / 6.16 patterns/s | 4.07 s / 19.66 patterns/s |
| Step 4 fitting only, six residual-indexed/refined points | 7.30 s | 1.28 s |

The Step 3 measurement includes worker startup, input loading, projection, fitting, transfer and storage; it excludes dictionary indexing/refinement. Step 4 inputs were generated by actually indexing the residuals against the saved 304,053-pattern dictionary and refining the five retained candidates. The six fits with staged plus joint refinement took 2.28 seconds. Both new Step 4 methods improved the fit on five points and had a small NCC decrease on one point relative to the previous search; joint refinement did not worsen the corresponding staged residual.

Validation also exercised parallel Step 4 with three workers, preserved nonnegative component coefficients and unit-sum fractions, and saved/reopened a temporary workflow. Residual and mixture images reconstructed exactly after reopening. The source patterns, source workflow and dictionary were read without modification.

All **146 automated tests passed**, covering weighted/masked objective agreement, degenerate inputs, custom bounds, shared-gain mixture behavior, exact cache reads, cleanup, selection ordering/duplicates, conditioning/binning, fixed and varying PCs, method routing and workflow persistence. A native Tk smoke check passed for both selectors, their shared state, label width, saved default and shutdown.

Reproduce the Step 3 measurement with [`step3_pipeline.py`](../benchmarks/step3_pipeline.py), selecting `--fit-method staged` or `--fit-method staged_joint`. [`cpu_workflow_validation.py`](../benchmarks/cpu_workflow_validation.py) exercises residual indexing/refinement, the Step 4 comparison and workflow reconstruction. It uses the six indices prepared by [`step3_prepare.py`](../benchmarks/step3_prepare.py). Raw measurements are in [`benchmarks/results/cpu_20260909`](../benchmarks/results/cpu_20260909/).

Metal/GPU execution is not implemented.
